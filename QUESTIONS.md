# Questions

## Q-001

### Question

What is the smallest possible set of concepts required to describe any adaptive change process?

### Status

Open

### Discussion

During the exploration of this question we realized that we may be asking the wrong question.

Instead of starting with concepts such as *Vision*, *Goal*, *Challenge* and *Mission*, we should first identify the underlying phenomena that exist in every adaptive change process.

A concept is something we invent.

A phenomenon is something that exists whether we choose to model it or not.

The goal is therefore not to discover the minimum set of concepts, but the minimum set of **primitive phenomena** required to describe adaptive change.

Only after identifying these phenomena should we introduce concepts that describe them.

This may lead to a distinction between:

- Primitive phenomena
- Primitive concepts
- Derived concepts

where concepts such as *Goal*, *Mission* or *Challenge* may ultimately prove to be higher-level abstractions rather than fundamental building blocks.

### Decision

## Decision

The current direction is to postpone defining primitive concepts until the underlying phenomena of adaptive change have been explored.

The next step is therefore to investigate the smallest possible adaptive loop that can exist, independent of any specific domain or terminology.

The hypothesis is that higher-level concepts such as Vision, Goal, Challenge and Mission may emerge naturally from this more fundamental model rather than being treated as primitive concepts themselves.

No

# Q-002 — Can adaptive change be modeled as a transformation of identity?

## Question

Can the core of an adaptive change process be represented by the transformation:

Identity → Obstacles & Opportunities → Vision / Ideal State

where the Ideal State represents a desired future Identity?

If so, are Identity, Obstacles, Opportunities and Ideal State sufficient as the fundamental building blocks, or is something essential missing?

---

## Motivation

Our exploration of primitive concepts has produced an increasingly large set of candidates:

- Current State
- Desired State
- Observation
- Decision
- Action
- Outcome
- Vision
- Goal
- Challenge
- Mission
- Opportunity
- etc.

This may indicate that we are decomposing the model too early.

An alternative hypothesis is that adaptive change can be understood primarily as a transformation from one Identity to another.

The current Identity describes what exists now.

The desired Identity describes what should exist in the future.

Obstacles and Opportunities describe factors that affect the possible transition between the two.

This may provide a much simpler and more intuitive foundation.

---

## Initial Hypothesis

A change process can be represented as:

    Current Identity
          │
          ├── Obstacles
          │
          ├── Opportunities
          │
          ▼
    Adaptive transition
          │
          ▼
    Desired Identity

The desired Identity may be expressed as a Vision or Ideal State.

"Vision" may therefore be a human-oriented representation of a desired future Identity rather than a fundamentally different type of entity.

---

## What is Identity?

Identity should not be understood merely as a name or description of an individual or organization.

A working hypothesis is that Identity is a structured representation of what something currently is, including relevant:

- characteristics
- capabilities
- resources
- relationships
- context
- values
- constraints
- behaviors
- states of relevant properties

Identity is therefore potentially a compact representation of a Current State.

The important question is which of these characteristics are actually fundamental and which are merely attributes or structures within Identity.

---

## What is a Future Identity?

An Ideal State may be understood as a desired future configuration of Identity.

For example:

Current Identity:
    "A person who cannot yet speak French."

Desired Identity:
    "A person who can comfortably conduct a conversation in French."

The change process is the transformation between these states.

Likewise:

Current Identity:
    "An organization with fragmented information systems."

Desired Identity:
    "An organization with an integrated information environment."

The Vision describes the desired future Identity in a form meaningful to humans.

---

## Obstacles and Opportunities

Obstacles and Opportunities may not be independent destinations or stages.

They may instead describe factors affecting the transition between identities.

An obstacle reduces or complicates the possibility of reaching the desired Identity.

An opportunity increases or enables the possibility of reaching the desired Identity.

This suggests that they may be relational concepts rather than fundamental objects.

---

## Important Challenge

The model must explain how transformation actually occurs.

Identity → Ideal Identity alone does not explain change.

There must be some mechanism through which the current Identity can be transformed.

Possible candidates include:

- Action
- Decision
- Learning
- Adaptation
- Interaction
- Experience
- Environmental change

It is not yet clear whether one or more of these must be primitive concepts, or whether they can themselves be represented as changes in Identity.

---

## Counterexamples to investigate

The model should be tested against very different types of change:

- Learning a language
- Becoming an electrician
- Starting a company
- Transforming an organization
- Recovering from a setback
- Responding to a crisis
- Scientific discovery
- Innovation
- Personal development

The model should also be tested against situations where:

- the desired state changes during the process
- new opportunities appear
- obstacles disappear
- the current Identity changes unintentionally
- multiple possible future Identities exist
- several actors interact
- the environment changes independently

---

## Relation to Q-001

Q-001 asks:

> What are the primitive phenomena of adaptive change?

Q-002 explores whether one of those fundamental phenomena can be described as a transformation of Identity.

The questions are therefore related but distinct.

---

## Identity as a Composite State

Identity may not be a primitive object with a fixed universal structure.

Instead, Identity may be a context-dependent configuration composed of relevant building blocks, capabilities, properties, relationships or other measurable characteristics.

The composition of Identity may therefore differ between domains.

For example, the relevant building blocks of:

- a person,
- a football action,
- an organisation,
- a software system,
- a crisis-response capability

may be completely different.

The potentially universal part is not the identity structure itself, but the process of identifying which characteristics are relevant and how changes in those characteristics contribute to a transition towards a desired Identity.

### Improvement Granularity

A transition may consist of changes to one or several identity building blocks.

The engine should investigate whether it is possible to identify the smallest meaningful improvement that produces measurable progress towards the desired Identity.

This suggests that transitions may operate at different scales:

- a single building block,
- several interacting building blocks,
- the whole Identity.

### Non-intrusive Observation

If relevant identity parameters can be observed continuously without disturbing the system, these observations can accompany every transition loop.

This creates the possibility of evaluating the complete journey retrospectively.

After reaching an Ideal State, the system could analyse the accumulated observations and identify which building blocks, transitions, opportunities or combinations were most influential in producing the change.

This is a hypothesis and requires further investigation.

### Decision as a Transition

A Transition does not necessarily consist of a sequence of actions.

In some situations, the smallest meaningful transition is a decision or
commitment.

A decision may:

- select one path among alternatives,
- establish or change an Intent,
- commit an Identity to a desired future,
- enable a large number of subsequent transitions,
- or deliberately select non-action / waiting.

The amount of subsequent work does not determine the conceptual size of
the Transition.

This should remain a hypothesis to consider during further modelling.

## Conclusion

The Identity → Transition → Identity model is currently considered sufficiently robust to serve as the initial working model for the project.

The model appears to be applicable across very different domains, including individual development, organisations, events, communities and systemic change.

The current working model is:

    Identity N
        │
        ├── Opportunities
        ├── Obstacles / Constraints
        ├── Motivation
        │
        ▼
    Transition
        │
        ▼
    Identity N+1
        │
        ▼
    Feedback / Observation
        │
        └──────────────→ next transition

Identity is understood as a context-dependent configuration of relevant
building blocks, capabilities, resources, relationships, characteristics
and other properties.

The building blocks of an Identity are not assumed to be universal.
They depend on what is being modelled.

A Transition is the mechanism through which one Identity becomes another.
A Transition may affect one or many Identity building blocks and may affect
multiple identities simultaneously.

A Mission / Task / Assignment is considered a possible concrete mechanism
for inducing a Transition.

Opportunities are considered particularly important. They may arise from
available capabilities, resources, relationships or possibilities, and may
be discovered through matchmaking between what identities have, can provide,
seek or need.

Matchmaking may therefore be a central mechanism for discovering
Opportunities, especially when many identities are available in the system.

Obstacles and Constraints are considered factors that restrict or complicate
possible transitions. They do not necessarily need to be "solved"; a viable
transition may instead be found around, through, or together with them.

Conflicting desired identities or visions are considered a possible form of
constraint or obstacle. The model does not currently require a separate
primitive concept for conflict.

Some constraints may be normative or external to the immediate identities
and transitions, such as laws, regulations, safety requirements or other
conditions that cannot simply be negotiated away.

Motivation is considered a driver of the transition loop rather than
necessarily an independent Identity building block. The size and nature of
a transition may affect motivation: transitions that are too difficult may
reduce motivation, while transitions that are too trivial may fail to
provide meaningful progress.

Feedback and observation allow the system to continuously reassess the
current Identity and determine what transition may be useful next.

The model does not require the entire path from Current Identity to Desired
Identity to be known in advance. Backcasting may be used to identify
possible intermediate identities, but the actual path is discovered
iteratively through the transition loop.

The desired Identity may also change during the process.

This means that the system is not primarily a planner that executes a
predefined path. It is a navigation system that continuously discovers
and supports meaningful transitions.

### Remaining Questions

The model is not considered proven.

The next phase is to stress-test it against increasingly complex cases,
including:

- individual development
- multi-actor cooperation
- organisational transformation
- systemic change
- conflicting desired identities
- changing visions
- emergent collective identities
- unintended consequences
- normative and legal constraints
- situations where the desired future is initially unclear

The model should only be changed when these tests reveal a fundamental
phenomenon that cannot be represented adequately by the current structure.

See [model stress test](model-stress-test-result.md).
