# Application Experience

## Status

**Discovery / Prototype**

## 1. Enter through a situation, not a profile wizard

Do not begin with a long sequence such as:

- What type of user are you?
- Are you an individual or organisation?
- What is your role?
- What industry are you in?
- What are your goals?

Instead begin with:

> **What are you working with right now?**

The answer can reveal whether the user is acting for themselves, an
organisation, several organisations, a community, or another Thing.

## 2. Working scope

The user has a current **working scope**: what are we looking at right now?

Examples:

```text
My next career step
Our football team
Our café
The district project
Öland Harvest Festival
The cooperation between these three organisations
```

The scope can change at any time.

This avoids a permanent onboarding choice such as "Individual" versus
"Organisation".

## 3. Progressive identification

The system learns relationships through ordinary use.

Example:

> "I'm helping a local property company make the district more attractive."

The model can represent:

```text
Person
    acts-with / acts-for → Property Company
    works-on → District
```

Later the system may ask:

> "Should we include the property company as a participant?"

That is a contextual question, not an account-type question.

## 4. Multiple participants are normal

The user can introduce:

```text
me
our organisation
another organisation
a partner
a customer
a community
a public authority
```

without changing workflow.

## 5. Reveal complexity progressively

Start simple. Reveal Things, relationships, configurations, opportunities,
constraints, projections, transitions and missions only when useful.

## 6. Five-part experience

### Situation

What is currently understood? The user can correct, remove or add information.

### Direction

Explore what matters, motivation, intent, desired change and uncertainty.

"I don't know yet" is valid.

### Explore

Show possible configurations, opportunities, constraints, relationships,
matches and continuation trajectory.

### Choose

Compare a small number of meaningful alternatives, including continuation.

### Transition

Turn the selected direction into the next meaningful Mission or Decision,
then observe and return to Situation.

## 7. No dashboard-first design

Do not initially overwhelm the user with graphs, KPIs, maps, relationship
networks, task lists or AI panels. These can become useful views later.

The primary interface is the current situation and the next meaningful step.
