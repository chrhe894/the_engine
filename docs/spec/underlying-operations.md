# Underlying Operations

## Status

**Discovery — Phase 1**

This document explores what different methods actually do beneath their
terminology.

The purpose is not to combine methodologies, but to identify recurring
operations that may form a useful language for the engine.

---

## First working extraction

A deliberately provisional set:

1. **Observe** — establish what appears to be happening.
2. **Frame** — determine what matters in the current context.
3. **Imagine** — describe possible different situations.
4. **Explore** — investigate ways between the current situation and possible futures.
5. **Evaluate** — assess alternatives and implications.
6. **Choose** — select a way forward or deliberately choose non-action.
7. **Act** — induce a transition in reality.
8. **Observe again** — determine what actually changed.

These are **not yet primitives**. We expect to reduce, split or reorganise
them as we test them.

## Observe

Establish what appears to be happening.

This can include:

- collecting observations
- inspecting the current situation
- establishing a baseline
- receiving feedback
- verifying what happened

Observation may itself require a Mission.

The result is information about the situation, not necessarily an
interpretation of what it means.

## Frame

Determine what matters for the transition being considered.

This can include:

- identifying the relevant Identity
- establishing Context
- determining scope
- identifying the desired direction
- establishing assumptions
- deciding what question is currently worth asking

Framing may change as new observations appear.

## Imagine

Describe possible different situations.

Examples:

- Vision
- Ideal State
- Desired Identity
- candidate future
- scenario
- possible outcome

The future does not need to be completely specified.

An uncertain or emerging Intent can also be represented here.

## Explore

Investigate what could connect the current situation with possible futures.

Two complementary modes are particularly important.

### Analysis

Break the situation apart:

- identify missing capabilities
- identify dependencies
- identify constraints
- identify causes
- identify risks
- identify gaps
- identify components

### Synthesis

Put things together:

- combine capabilities
- combine resources
- discover cooperation
- connect people and organisations
- discover unused capacity
- create new configurations
- identify matchmaking possibilities

Analysis and synthesis are complementary. A system that only analyses may
become trapped in problem-solving; a system that only synthesises may
overlook real constraints.

## Evaluate

Assess possible transitions.

Possible dimensions include:

- relevance to the desired direction
- feasibility
- effort
- risk
- constraints
- available opportunities
- motivation
- dependencies
- expected effects
- reversibility
- timing

Evaluation does not necessarily produce one objectively correct answer.

## Choose

Select a way forward.

This may mean:

- choose a candidate transition
- choose between alternatives
- prioritise
- make a decision
- commit
- deliberately choose to wait
- deliberately choose non-action

A decision can itself constitute a meaningful Transition.

The user remains the final decision-maker when AI generates or evaluates
alternatives.

## Act

Induce a transition in reality.

Examples:

- perform a Mission
- complete a Task
- implement a change
- establish a relationship
- allocate a resource
- make a decision
- deliver something
- change a configuration

The smallest meaningful action is often the most useful next step.

## Observe again

Determine what actually happened.

The result may be:

- intended change
- partial change
- unexpected change
- no meaningful change
- new Opportunity
- new Constraint
- changed Motivation
- changed Identity
- changed Intent

The next iteration starts from this new situation.

---

## Operations are not levels

Strategic / tactical / operative should not be mapped one-to-one onto these
operations.

The same operation can occur at different levels.

For example, Observe can be strategic, tactical or operative.

Likewise, Choose can happen at any level.

The operations describe **what is being done with information**.

Strategic / tactical / operative describe **the level or resolution of what
is being considered**.

These are different dimensions.

## Operations are not methods

OODA, ABCD, Backcasting and other methods may package several operations
into named steps.

The purpose here is to identify recurring activities underneath their
terminology, not to translate each method literally.

## Relationship to the change model

A working representation is:

```text
CURRENT IDENTITY / SITUATION
          │
        OBSERVE
          │
         FRAME
          │
        IMAGINE
          │
        EXPLORE
       ↙       ↘
   ANALYSIS   SYNTHESIS
       ↘       ↙
        EVALUATE
           │
         CHOOSE
           │
          ACT
           │
       TRANSITION
           │
NEW IDENTITY / SITUATION
           │
     OBSERVE AGAIN
           │
           └──────→ next iteration
```

This is only a working representation. The system must not force every
transition through every operation.

## Current hypotheses

### H-001 — A small set of operations may underlie many methods

Different methodologies may package recurring operations differently.

### H-002 — Analysis and synthesis are complementary

Analysis decomposes.

Synthesis combines.

Both are required for useful navigation.

### H-003 — Operations and levels are different dimensions

Strategic / tactical / operative should not be collapsed into the operation
model.

### H-004 — The complete sequence is not mandatory

A user may already know the desired state. A decision may be the next
transition. A Mission may produce an observation that immediately changes
the framing.

The system should adapt to the situation.

### H-005 — The user selects the way forward

AI may propose, analyse, compare and explain.

The user chooses the direction.

## Open questions

1. Can Observe and Frame be reduced to a smaller operation?
2. Is Imagine distinct from Explore?
3. Is Evaluate part of Explore or a separate operation?
4. Is Choose a special case of Transition?
5. Is Act necessary as a separate operation, or simply the mechanism through
   which a Transition occurs?
6. Where does Motivation belong?
7. Where does learning belong?
8. Where does sensemaking belong?
9. How do Decisions relate to Choose and Transition?
10. How do Vision and Intent relate to Imagine?
11. Can matchmaking be expressed entirely through Explore?
12. Can all known methods be explained using these operations without losing
    something essential?
13. What is the smallest useful set?

## Next test

Apply the candidate operations to:

- OODA
- ABCD
- Backcasting
- strategic / tactical / operative
- What → How → With What → Verify / Validate
- conventional problem solving
- opportunity / cooperation
- the "I don't know" exploration example
- a decision-only transition

The purpose is not to prove that the methods are identical.

The purpose is to discover where the underlying operations genuinely differ.
