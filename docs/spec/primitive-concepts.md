# Primitive Concepts

## Purpose

This document explores the smallest possible set of concepts required to describe adaptive change.

A concept should be primitive only if it represents a universal phenomenon that cannot reasonably be expressed using simpler concepts.

Primitive concepts should be:

- universal
- domain-independent
- necessary
- implementation-independent

A useful human-facing concept does not automatically need to be a fundamental information type.

## Current conclusion

> **No new primitive concept is currently justified.**

This is a working conclusion for Model v0.1, not a claim that the ontology is permanently complete.

## Current candidate semantic forms

The current information-model working hypothesis uses:

- Thing
- Statement
- Observation
- Intention
- Relationship
- Context
- Transition

`Mission` is currently treated as a concrete mechanism for inducing a Transition rather than necessarily a primitive information type.

The following are currently treated as higher-level interpretations, perspectives or mechanisms:

- Identity
- Identity Brick
- Vision
- Ideal State
- Goal
- Opportunity
- Obstacle
- Constraint
- Motivation
- Decision
- Commitment
- Mission
- Definition of Done
- Evidence
- KPI
- Matchmaking

## Why the current set survives

### Thing

A generic referent for people, organisations, places, events, groups, resources and other entities.

### Statement

A generic way to express meaningful information about Things and relationships.

### Observation

Information obtained about reality after or during change.

### Intention

A desired direction, future configuration, outcome or change. It may emerge gradually through exploration.

### Relationship

Needed for cooperation, dependencies, opportunities and matchmaking across Things.

### Context

Needed because information and Identity are relevant within time, place, situation, perspective and other conditions.

### Transition

Meaningful change between configurations. It can affect one or many Identities. A decision may itself be the smallest meaningful Transition.

## Concepts deliberately not promoted to primitives

### Opportunity and Obstacle

Two complementary ways of reasoning about factors affecting transition. The obstacle-oriented approach tends to analyse and decompose; the opportunity-oriented approach tends to synthesise capabilities, resources and relationships into possibilities and cooperation.

### Motivation

Influences transition selection and size but is not currently required as a fundamental information form.

### Decision

Important in the method, especially because choosing a direction or deliberately choosing non-action can itself be a Transition. It is not yet necessary as a separate primitive.

### Definition of Done

Describes conditions for considering a Transition complete. It may be expressible through existing information forms.

### Evidence

A useful working term. The system may obtain observations through GPS, human confirmation, surveys, assessments, application mechanisms or other means. The mechanism should not dictate the information model.

## Stress-test conclusion

The model has been considered against:

- individual development
- newcomer → software developer
- football
- Öland's Harvest Festival
- business and district development
- crisis-management/systemic-change scenarios

The current cases do not justify another primitive.

Two areas remain useful for targeted testing:

1. conflicting desired futures
2. external/normative constraints

The current hypothesis is that both can be represented as constraints or relationships affecting possible transitions.

## Model v0.1 principle

> **Do not add a primitive concept merely because implementation becomes inconvenient.**

If implementation reveals a phenomenon that genuinely cannot be represented, return to the model and investigate it as a new question.

The model is intentionally provisional and falsifiable.
